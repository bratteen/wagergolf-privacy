## What is Stableford?

Stableford is a points system rather than straight stroke counting. You earn points for how well you play each hole relative to par, adjusted for your handicap. The clever part is that a genuinely bad hole simply scores zero. You never have to write down a seven — you pick the ball up and move on. That makes Stableford quick, kind to the beginner and ideal for mixed groups.

The scale continues upwards for the genuinely rare holes. An albatross is worth 5 points, and a hole-in-one on a par 5 a full 6.

## How it works with handicap

Stableford is nearly always played on net score. You receive a number of strokes based on your course handicap, allocated to the hardest holes according to the course's stroke index. On those holes you get an extra margin before your points start to drop. A gross bogey on a hole where you have a stroke becomes a net par, and scores 2 points.

It is this allocation that lets a 28-handicapper and a 9-handicapper play the same round and still meet on equal terms.

## What counts as a good score?

36 points is the benchmark. It means you averaged a net par on every hole — in other words, played exactly to your handicap. Get above 36 and you have had a genuinely good day. Most club rounds land somewhere between 30 and 34.

## When does Stableford suit?

Stableford shines when the group plays off different standards, or when you want to keep the pace up. Because a dreadful hole only scores zero, nobody has to write down a nine — you pick up and move on. That is why almost every club competition and social round is played as Stableford. If you want straight stroke counting where every shot counts, choose [stroke play]({% guideUrl "slaggolf" %}) instead. See also [Stableford vs stroke play](/en/game-formats/stableford-vs-stroke-play/) for a full comparison of the two formats.

## Common misunderstandings

- **You do not need to play all 18 holes for it to count.** Points are independent per hole, so an abandoned round can still be settled on the holes you managed.
- **Points are scored on net, not gross.** A bogey on a hole where you have a stroke is a net par and scores 2 points. Plenty of players underestimate their score because they count gross.
- **Zero points is not a failure.** It is the entire idea of the format. Pick the ball up and save your energy for the next hole.
