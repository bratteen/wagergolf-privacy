## What is a Greensome?

Greensome sits between a scramble and a Foursome, and may be the most balanced team format of all. Both players tee off, which removes the dread of a single bad drive, but from there you share a ball and play alternate shots as in a Foursome. That gives you both security and tactics.

## How to play a Greensome

- Both players on the team tee off.
- The team picks the better of the two drives.
- The player whose tee shot was not chosen plays the next stroke.
- From there you alternate shot by shot until the ball is holed.

## Greensome variations

- **Irish Greensome:** both players play their own balls a stroke or two into the hole before you decide which to continue with. More chances early, but you have to play two balls for longer.
- **Standard Greensome:** you choose straight after the tee shot, which keeps the pace up.

## How it is worked out

Two teams go head to head on the team's combined score, as a match or on the total. Choosing the better drive makes a Greensome kinder than a Foursome, but the alternating ball keeps the tension.

The team handicap in a Greensome is 60 per cent of the lower handicap plus 40 per cent of the higher.

## Tactics: more than just the longest drive

The temptation is always to choose the longest tee shot, but think one shot further. A slightly shorter drive sitting on the fairway with a clear line to the green is often worth more than a long one in the semi-rough. Think about who plays next, too: choose one player's drive and it is their partner who hits the approach, so pick the tee shot that hands your stronger player the ball they actually want.

## When does a Greensome suit?

A Greensome is often the best team format for mixed groups and competitions. It removes the dread of a single bad drive, because both of you play one, but keeps the tension of the shared ball. If you want something gentler still, play a scramble. If you want the hardest and most demanding version, a Foursome is the answer.
