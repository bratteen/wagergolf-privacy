## What is closest to the pin?

Closest to the pin is the classic par 3 bet found at every club competition. On the chosen short holes it is all about precision: whoever puts their tee shot nearest the flag wins the pot. It turns every par 3 into a little championship of its own, and gives even the shortest hitter a chance to shine.

## How to play

- Choose which holes count before the round, usually par 3s.
- Everyone hits their tee shot at the green.
- Whoever's ball finishes closest to the pin wins the hole.
- Normally the ball has to be on the green to count.

## How the pot is decided

Each chosen hole has its own pot: the stake times the number of players. The winner of the hole takes the whole pot for that hole. Play closest to the pin on several holes and there are several separate pots to fight over during the round.

## A worked example

Say there are four of you and you agree a stake of 50 on two chosen par 3s, the 7th and the 16th. Each hole gets its own pot: 50 times four players is 200 per hole.

On the 7th, Anna puts her tee shot closest to the pin, with the ball on the green. She takes the whole 200 pot. Because she put in 50 herself, her net is +150 for that hole, and the other three are 50 down each.

On the 16th everyone misses the green except Erik, who wins the second pot the same way: +150 for Erik, 50 down each for the other three.

- Pot per hole: stake × number of players (50 × 4 = 200)
- The winner takes the whole pot; the net is the pot minus their own stake
- Nobody on the green, no winner: the stakes stay put and no money moves

Add both holes together: Anna wins the 7th (+150) but loses her stake on the 16th (−50), netting +100. Erik the same the other way round, netting +100. The other two lose their stake on both holes, −50 plus −50, so −100 each. It balances out: +100, +100, −100, −100. Wager Golf works out the pots hole by hole and settles up.
